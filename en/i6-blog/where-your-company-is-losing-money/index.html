Demand Forecasting

Where is your company losing money because decisions are happening too late?

Artificial intelligence is already on retail's radar.

AI appears in more personalized campaigns, product recommendations, automated service, behavior analysis, and new forms of customer relationship. All of this has value. But there is a less visible, and often more critical, layer where AI can have a direct impact on results: demand, inventory, and supply chain.

A recent CartaCapital article about the use of AI in retail highlighted precisely this point. Many companies have advanced in marketing and customer service but still have a great opportunity in demand forecasting, logistics, and inventory management.

And this opportunity is not just technological.

It's financial.

Because much of the money a company loses doesn't appear as an obvious error. It appears as a sale that didn't happen. A product that was out of stock. Excess inventory. A purchasing decision made too late. An allocation that didn't keep up with real demand. A margin pressured by discounts that could have been avoided.
In retail, deciding late is expensive.

The problem starts before the stockout.

When a product is missing from the shelf, the problem has already occurred. The sale was lost. The customer may have chosen another brand. The campaign effort may have generated demand for an unavailable item. The operation, at this point, is already reacting.

The same happens with excess inventory. When the company realizes it bought too much or allocated poorly, part of the loss has already formed. Capital is tied up. Space is occupied. Future margin is threatened. And the correction usually comes through discounts, emergency transfers, or increased operational costs.

The important question is not just, "what happened?" The right question is: "what signs already indicated this was going to happen?"

This is the difference between a reactive operation and a predictive operation.

Retail doesn't suffer from a lack of data.

Most companies already have a lot of data: sales by SKU; inventory turnover by store; available inventory; stockout history; margin by category; campaigns; price; channel; region; seasonality; lead time; purchasing behavior; sell-in; sell-out...

The challenge isn't to generate more information. The challenge is to transform information into decisions before the problem turns into a loss.

When these signals are scattered across systems, reports, and spreadsheets, the company can glimpse the past. But it takes time to anticipate what needs to be done now.

And, in a market with pressured margins, demand volatility, and increasingly complex operations, this delay impacts cash flow.

Predictive AI shifts the decision-making center

Predictive AI applied to retail allows moving from a logic based solely on history and averages to a logic guided by signals, patterns, and actionable recommendations. This means forecasting demand behavior with greater granularity by product, store, channel, and period.

But forecasting, alone, is not enough.

The real value emerges when forecasting helps to make better decisions.

  • What to buy.
  • How much to buy.
  • Where to allocate.
  • When to replenish.
  • Which SKU has the highest risk of stockout.
  • Where excess inventory is forming.
  • Which store needs adjustment.
  • Which decision best protects revenue, margin, turnover, and service level.

This is the most important frontier of AI in retail: moving from diagnosis to operational decision.

The direct impact on the business

When a company anticipates demand more accurately, it reduces the chance of losing sales due to product stockouts.

When it improves allocation, it places inventory where there is greater potential for turnover.

When it identifies excess before it consolidates, it avoids tied-up capital and reduces the need for aggressive discounts.

When it connects demand, inventory, margin, and service level, it stops making isolated decisions and starts operating with a more intelligent business vision.

The impact appears on four main fronts:

  • Revenue, because there are fewer lost sales due to stockouts.
  • Margin, because the company reduces expensive corrections, markdowns, emergency decisions, and makes capital more efficient.
  • Working capital, because it buys and distributes with greater precision.
  • Operational efficiency, because decision-making becomes faster, more granular, and guided by real behavioral data.

Ultimately, AI only generates value when it changes an important decision.

And few decisions are as important for retail as forecasting demand, replenishing correctly, and protecting margins.

The question leaders should be asking now

Many companies still start the conversation about AI by asking what tool they should use. This should not be the first question.

The more strategic question is:

Where are we losing money because our decisions happen too late?

The answer may lie:

  1. In the stockout of high-turnover products;
  2. In excess inventory in slow-moving categories;
  3. In incorrect allocation among stores, distribution centers, e-commerce, and marketplaces;
  4. In the difficulty of adjusting assortments by region;
  5. In the delay in perceiving changes in consumer behavior.

Or simply in the gap between what the data shows and what the operation can execute.

When this question is answered clearly, AI ceases to be a generic innovation project and becomes a concrete lever for efficiency, growth, and profitability.

The next frontier of AI in retail will be operational

AI applied to marketing and customer service will continue to evolve. It improves relationships, personalization, and productivity.

But one of the biggest opportunities in retail lies behind the scenes, in:

  • Better forecasting.
  • Better purchasing.
  • Better allocation.
  • Better replenishment.
  • Avoiding stockouts.
  • Reducing excess.
  • Protecting margins.

The next frontier of artificial intelligence in retail will not just be selling more. It will be preventing sales already created by demand from being lost due to a decision made too late.

It is precisely at this point that infinity6 operates: transforming demand and supply data into predictive, actionable decisions connected to the real business impact.

Because the value of AI is not in appearing innovative, but in helping the company act before a loss occurs.
