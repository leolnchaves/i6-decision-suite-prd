Purchase intention signals: how to turn behavior into conversion before the competitor

Your customer may not say they intend to buy. But they are already leaving clues.

A specific search. Returning to the same page. Comparing products. Checking price or availability. Separately, each behavior says little. Together and in the right context, they reveal an undeclared intention — and an opportunity that can quickly disappear.

Intention signals are behaviors and contexts that indicate the probability of a customer buying, hiring, or responding to an offer. For technology companies, financial institutions, and consumer goods industries, the challenge is to interpret them while there is still time to influence the decision.

Consumers no longer follow a predictable funnel

The customer journey is no longer linear. A survey published by Google in April 2026 shows that Search and YouTube are present in 84% of journeys where Brazilians discovered a new brand, product, or retailer. Furthermore, 86% state they are open to trying new brands while researching.

AI makes this behavior even more complex. Queries in Google's AI Mode are, on average, three times longer, indicating more contextual searches. Discovery, comparison, and decision begin to happen in the same environment.

In practice, intention appears before conversion and changes rapidly. A person may have a high propensity but need installment payments; seek a discount even with a high income; or be ready to convert without a coupon.

Therefore, intention should not be confused with a click or demographic profile.

It is worth noting that identifying intention does not mean exposing personal data or monitoring consumers indiscriminately. Propensity models can operate with anonymized identifiers, aggregated data, and behavioral variables, respecting purpose, minimization, and governance. In regulated sectors, the explainability of the recommendation is as important as its accuracy.

An isolated signal is not enough. The combination is what generates intelligence

The most valuable signals combine:

  1. Recent behavior: searches, comparisons, abandonments, and returns.
  2. Context: channel, category, occasion, price, stock, and moment.
  3. Ability to act: financial condition, credit, availability, and necessary incentive.

According to Meio & Mensagem, for Black Friday 2026, 48% of consumers who intended to buy already had a defined list and 44% had started researching prices. At the same time, 51% had their income committed.

The intention existed, but conversion still depended on the right condition.

On Father's Day, Serasa Experian identified 11.7 million Brazilians with a higher propensity to buy. Of these, 57.8% were discount hunters — three times the average — although 26.9% had an income above R$ 8,000.

Income and intention, alone, do not reveal which offer will be most efficient.

What intention signals change in each market

In technology, recurring use, price inquiries, and integrations or a drop in engagement can indicate a propensity for upgrade, cross-sell, or churn.

In the financial market, simulations, transactions, affinities, and responses to campaigns help identify who might take out credit, insurance, investments, or other products — and what argument to use.

In consumer goods, searches, sell-out, recurrence, price, availability, and seasonality anticipate purchases and changes in demand by SKU, region, or POS. The signal guides marketing, assortment, supply, and pricing.

In all these sectors, the gain is in transforming signals into decisions: who to activate, when, with what offer, channel, and incentive.

The cost of interpreting intention too late

Generic campaigns, static clusters, and rules like “abandoned cart, send coupon” lead to three losses: investment in audiences with low propensity, discounts for those who would buy without incentive, and a delayed approach to those who were ready to convert.

Predictive AI changes this logic. Instead of just asking what the customer did, it estimates what they are likely to do now and prescribes the most promising action.

If your company already collects customer, transaction, and channel data, but still decides based on the past, the opportunity is probably already within the operation.

The next step is to discover which signals anticipate conversion — and how much value can be captured by acting sooner.

Frequently asked questions about intention signals

What are intention signals?

They are behaviors and contexts that help estimate the likelihood of a person buying, hiring, or responding to an offer, such as searches, recurring visits, comparisons, simulations, cart, abandonment, and purchase history.

Which signals indicate greater purchase intention?

The strongest ones involve increasing commitment: returning to the product, comparing conditions, checking availability, simulating a contract, initiating checkout, or negotiating an offer. Their strength depends on recency, frequency, sequence, and context.

What is the difference between segmentation and purchase propensity?

Segmentation groups similar people. Propensity estimates the probability of a specific action in a given context and moment. Two people from the same segment can have different probabilities.

Is it possible to identify intention in anonymous visitors?

Yes. Session, origin, device, navigation, search, and recurrence can indicate intention even without login or identified history.
