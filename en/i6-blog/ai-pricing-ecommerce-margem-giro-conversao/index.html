Price & Elasticity

Right price on the spreadsheet, wrong margin in the result: the new challenge for e-commerce pricing

Understand how AI Pricing helps e-commerce stores transform pricing into a dynamic decision, connecting margin, turnover, conversion, stock, and governance.

In e-commerce, a price can be technically correct and commercially wrong at the same time.

It can respect the minimum margin. It can be aligned with the commercial table. It can seem competitive when compared to the competitor. It can have been approved in a meeting and still leave money on the table.

This happens because price is not an isolated variable. It is a decision that changes according to the context.

It changes with inventory. It changes with the elasticity of each SKU. It changes with the category's turnover. It changes with the origin of traffic. It changes with purchase intent. It changes with seasonality. It changes with competitive pressure. It changes with the margin target.

When all these signals are analyzed slowly, manually, or with little granularity, the problem rarely appears as a "pricing error." It appears in the result: compressed margin, unnecessary discount, stagnant inventory, late markdown, lost conversion, and trapped capital.

The issue is that many companies still treat pricing as a table, when it has already become a financial lever.

Why the traditional pricing model fell short

For a long time, pricing based on history, minimum margin, periodic meetings, and punctual monitoring of competition seemed sufficient.

But e-commerce operates at a different pace.

Demand changes throughout the day. Competition adjusts prices more frequently. Consumers compare options in seconds. Inventory ages while the decision is still being discussed. Campaigns, channels, and browsing behaviors create different contexts for the same product.

In this scenario, deciding prices only by average, history, or fixed rule creates distortions.

An SKU with high demand and low price sensitivity may be giving up margin unnecessarily. Another, with pressured inventory and low turnover speed, may need a more precise markdown strategy. A third may have good conversion in a certain session context, but lose revenue when treated with the same logic applied to the entire catalog.

The problem is not a lack of data.
The problem is transforming data into decisions before the opportunity passes.

The real tension: margin, turnover, and conversion

Every pricing decision involves a trade-off.

If the price goes up, the margin may improve, but conversion may fall. If the price goes down, conversion may go up, but the margin may be destroyed. If the product doesn't move, inventory ages. If the markdown comes in too early, the company teaches the market to wait for discounts. If it comes in too late, the cost has already been absorbed by the operation.

That's why the question "what price should we set?" is limited.

The most important question is:

What pricing decision generates the best possible result for this product, in this context, respecting the business constraints?

This change in question is what separates operational pricing from intelligent pricing.

What AI Pricing changes in practice

AI Pricing does not mean letting AI change prices without criteria.

It means using artificial intelligence to expand analysis and recommendation capabilities, considering more variables than a team could manually process with the same frequency and granularity.

In practice, AI can help estimate elasticity per SKU, project demand, simulate scenarios, identify opportunities for margin capture, calibrate markdown, recommend price ranges, and measure decision results before scaling.

The central point is that the recommendation needs to operate within the business rules.

Minimum margin, available inventory, commercial policy, competitive band, exposure rules, and governance are not details. They are constraints that define the safe decision space.

AI does not replace commercial strategy. It helps execute that strategy with more speed, precision, and traceability.

Three ways to apply AI Pricing in e-commerce

At infinity6, the vision of AI Pricing starts from three complementary fronts: margin, turnover, and conversion.

1. Price-to-Margin: capturing margin without breaking the commercial strategy

Not every product needs a discount to sell better. And not every competitive price maximizes results.

Price-to-Margin seeks to identify margin capture opportunities per SKU, considering elasticity, forecast, price and volume history, competition, inventory, and commercial restrictions.

The logic is simple: if two products have different behaviors, they should not be treated by the same rule.

With a more granular view, the company can understand where there is room to preserve or expand margin without compromising the commercial strategy.

2. Price-to-Turnover: accelerating turnover without turning discount into autopilot

Stagnant inventory is margin under pressure.

But solving turnover only with discounts can erode profitability. The challenge is to understand when, where, and with what intensity to adjust prices to move products without destroying value.

Price-to-Turnover considers product life cycle, sales velocity, seasonality, inventory, region, store, category, or cluster to recommend price and markdown rules with more precision.

The decision is no longer "lower price because it didn't sell" and becomes "which movement best preserves the balance between turnover and margin?"

3. Price-to-Conversion: deciding based on session context

In e-commerce, two sessions are never exactly alike.

A recurring user, coming from a specific campaign, browsing at a certain time, and demonstrating high purchase intent, does not have the same context as a new visitor, without history and without clear conversion signals.

Price-to-Conversion uses session signals, user history, channel origin, inventory, real-time demand, and commercial rules to support more contextual recommendations.

The goal is not simply to convert more. It is to increase conversion and revenue per session without giving up margin.

Governance is what makes AI applicable

The biggest objection to AI in pricing is legitimate: price is too sensitive to become an uncontrolled experiment.

Therefore, governance needs to be in the solution's design, not in the discourse.

Before recommending any action, the model must respect restrictions such as minimum margin, available inventory, commercial policy, and exposure rules. Then, the company needs to monitor actual versus recommended results, perform backtests, adjust assumptions, and recalibrate models as new data comes in.

This process avoids two common pitfalls: relying too much on a recommendation without explainability or limiting AI to a decorative layer, without real impact on the decision.

Well-implemented AI Pricing does not take control from the company.

It increases the quality of control.

How to know if your operation is ready for AI Pricing

The journey doesn't have to start with the entire catalog.

The best starting point is usually where there is the greatest tension between margin, turnover, and conversion.

Some questions help to find this cut:

  • Which categories have the greatest margin pressure?
  • Which SKUs are frequently discounted, but without clarity of necessity?
  • Where is inventory aging?
  • Where does markdown arrive too late?
  • Which products have high demand variation?
  • Where does decision still depend on spreadsheets and manual cycles?
  • What data already exists, but has not yet become actionable recommendations?

From there, it is possible to start with a pilot, validate impact, create governance, and scale.

The future of pricing is less reaction and more decision

E-commerce doesn't need another layer of complexity. It needs better decisions.

Pricing has ceased to be a routine of table updates. It has become a commercial intelligence system that connects data, margin, inventory, demand, conversion, and governance.

A company that decides prices too late loses money even when the system seems to be working.

And that's precisely where AI Pricing gains relevance: not as a generic promise of automation, but as a practical way to transform data into faster, measurable, and results-aligned commercial decisions.
